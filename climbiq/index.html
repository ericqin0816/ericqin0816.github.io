01 THE PROBLEM

A finish time leaves a lot out.

A race video contains more than a final number. Timing-light changes, audio cues, and athlete motion can help explain what happened during an attempt. Reviewing those signals manually means moving between frames, locating events, and trying to compare one recording with another.

As a speed climber, I wanted those tasks in one place: a way to inspect footage, review candidate timing markers, and compare attempts without sending the video to a server.

The design constraint: make the evidence easy to review. An uncertain event should be visible and correctable.

02 THE APPROACH

Automate the candidates.
Keep the review human.

ClimbIQ loads race recordings directly in the browser. It combines timing-light, audio, and athlete-motion evidence to generate candidate race events and split information, then gives the user tools to inspect frames and correct ambiguous markers.

  • Local-first analysis: video stays on-device.
  • Review and correction: inspect individual frames and adjust uncertain timing markers.
  • Repeatable comparison: save attempts locally, compare them, and export analysis data.

This makes the platform useful as a review tool without treating every automated detection as a confirmed result.

Light

Changes in timing lights provide candidate race-event evidence. Frame and pixel inspection support review of those changes.

Audio

Audio cues add timing evidence when available. Missing audio is part of the robustness testing rather than an assumption that every recording has a useful soundtrack.

Motion

MediaPipe Pose estimates athlete motion. Motion evidence helps interpret the race alongside light and audio cues. I integrated MediaPipe; I did not train its pose model.

04 ARCHITECTURE

The browser is the workspace.

A React and TypeScript interface brings video loading, analysis, frame review, and attempt comparison together. Vite supports the application build; Canvas, Web Audio, MediaPipe Pose, and WebAssembly support the browser-side analysis stack.

05 TESTING & ENGINEERING

Test the footage.
Test the messy states.

ClimbIQ was evaluated across 11 source recordings and 30 controlled variants. The testing covers altered footage as well as browser review behavior, with 126 frame seek/pixel checks and more than 230 noisy-state automated trials.

06 FAILURE CASES

Ambiguity is part of the interface.

The robustness conditions highlight the kinds of recordings a timing-analysis tool needs to handle: light evidence can be affected by exposure or compression, recordings may lack audio, and resizing, trimming, or frame-rate changes alter the available video evidence.

These are conditions tested, not a claim that every one produced a failure. When a detection is ambiguous, ClimbIQ lets the user inspect individual frames and correct the proposed timing marker.

The engineering choice: build review and correction tools around uncertain detections instead of hiding uncertainty behind a definitive-looking time.

07 RESULTS

A complete review workflow.

The result is a browser-based platform that brings video loading, candidate-event detection, frame inspection, marker correction, local attempt saves, comparison, and export into one workflow.

The strongest established evidence is the implemented workflow and the documented scope of testing. General timing accuracy has not been established in this case study.

08 LIMITATIONS

A tool for analysis, with limits.

  • Evaluation is bounded by the 11 source recordings and 30 controlled variants. It does not establish performance across every camera, setting, or race.
  • Candidate timing markers may need manual review and correction.
  • Motion estimates use an existing MediaPipe model; they are not a custom-trained climbing model.
  • No general timing-accuracy claim or certified-timing equivalence is made.
  • Saved attempts are local to the user’s device; analysis exports support keeping a copy elsewhere.

09 WHAT’S NEXT

More evidence. Better review.

The next direction is to strengthen validation and make review more useful for climbers. These are proposed next steps, rather than completed results.

DIRECTIONS TO EXPLORE
  • Expand the evaluation footage and capture more varied recording conditions.
  • Measure timing error against independently verified reference events.
  • Make ambiguous evidence and correction decisions easier to understand.
  • Refine attempt comparison based on feedback from climbers.
