CruxRNN

Independent computational neuroscience research · 2026

Climbing got me thinking about repetition: what changes when you practice one movement pattern at a time or mix several together? I reproduced a motor-learning study, then tested how the similarity between sequences changes the outcome in a small recurrent neural network.

Read the story →GitHub ↗
A quick look at the result +

The final experiment used 120 paired runs, or 240 trained networks. The average interleaving advantage in held-out action accuracy grew from 2.1 to 38.9 percentage points as sequences became less alike. This is a result about the model; the study did not test human learning or climbing performance.

RobustDrive

Independent machine learning research · In progress

I trained a neural network to steer in a driving simulator, then changed what it could see. The question was whether blur, shadows, or other visual changes would throw it off—and whether its uncertainty could help spot a bad prediction.

Read the story →GitHub ↗
Where the project is now +

The first recording has been evaluated across three models and 55 corrupted-image conditions. The simplest trained model performed best, and the dropout uncertainty score did not reliably catch large steering errors. The research page walks through those results and the next tests.

Solar + Battery Economics

Independent research · In progress

I’m building a model to work out when residential solar and a home battery become worth the cost for a Texas homeowner. I’m focusing on the break-even electricity rate and how it changes from one household to another.

What I’m working through +

The same system can make sense for one home and be a poor fit for another. I’m varying system cost, electricity use, utility rates, and battery assumptions to see which inputs move the break-even threshold the most.

The study is still in progress: literature review, methodology, data collection, sensitivity analysis, and validation. I don’t have a final result to share yet.

ClimbIQ

Creator & Engineer

A race time tells me how fast I climbed, but I still want to see where the seconds went. I built ClimbIQ to review my footage, find race events, and compare attempts without uploading the video.

Read the story →GitHub ↗
How it works +

Timing-light changes, audio cues, and MediaPipe Pose help suggest race events and split times. I can step through the footage and correct a marker when the signals disagree. Attempts save on the device and can be compared or exported.

Built with React, TypeScript, Vite, Canvas, Web Audio, and WebAssembly. Testing covered 11 source recordings, 30 altered video variants, 126 frame-seeking and pixel checks, and more than 230 automated trials under noisy conditions.

Council Check-in

Builder · Student Council

Taking attendance for a large council gets repetitive quickly. I built a check-in system where students scan their IDs and their attendance lands in the right place in Google Sheets.

The small details that mattered +

The setup uses a USB barcode scanner, a Chromebook, Google Sheets, and Google Apps Script. Members are rows and meetings are columns.

Most of the work was making ordinary mistakes manageable: duplicate scans, unknown IDs, the wrong meeting column, and accidental new rows.
